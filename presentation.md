# Queue Management System (QMS)

**A simple, fair way to call people in line — built for the City Civil Registrar’s Office of Maasin City.**

---

## Executive Summary

The Queue Management System helps an office serve people in order, without shouting names, guessing who is next, or arguing over the line.

Staff at the windows call the next number. A large screen in the waiting area shows which number is being served at which window, and a voice announcement tells the person it is their turn. Supervisors can start or reset the day’s queue, rename windows, and see how many people have been served.

The system is meant for day-to-day front-desk work: regular clients and priority clients (such as senior citizens, persons with disability, or pregnant women) wait in two separate lines. Numbers are issued in order so no one is skipped and no two windows call the same number.

**Main purpose:** keep the waiting line fair, clear, and calm — for the public and for the staff who serve them.

---

## Features

- **Fair numbering.** People are called in sequence. Regular numbers (1, 2, 3…) and priority numbers (P1, P2, P3…) run as two separate lines so priority clients can be served without losing their place among other priority clients.
- **Call next, or call priority.** Windows 1 and 2 can take the next regular number or the next priority number, depending on who should be served.
- **Separate Releasing Window.** When a request needs a document, Window 1 or 2 sends the client to the Releasing Window (Counter 3) instead of finishing them. The Releasing Window calls each client back as soon as their document is ready, in whatever order the documents are finished. If the client is not there, it can call them again or put them back on the list.
- **One person at a time per window.** A window finishes the current number before calling another, so staff are not juggling two clients at once.
- **Live public display.** A wall screen shows, at a glance, which number is being served at each window, plus a clock and how many people have been served so far.
- **Voice announcements.** When a number is called, the display speaks it out loud (for example: “Number 12, you may now proceed at Counter 2”, or “Number 12, your document is ready. Please proceed to the Releasing Window.”), so people do not have to watch the screen constantly.
- **Supervisor controls.** An authorized person starts the queue for the day, can reset it if needed, rename windows, and turn a window on or off (for lunch break, absence, or a closed lane).
- **Activity log.** Supervisors can see a running list of numbers called, completed, or cancelled, with time and window — useful for checking what happened during the day.
- **Day’s progress at a glance.** Totals for regular served, priority served, people currently being served, and the next number waiting.
- **Staff can see each other.** Each window also shows what the other windows are serving, so staff stay coordinated.
- **Protected supervisor actions.** Starting, resetting, renaming, and turning windows on or off require a PIN, so the queue cannot be changed by accident from a public or staff computer.
- **The day’s queue is not lost to a brief interruption.** If power or the computer restarts during office hours, the current numbers and log for that day come back. A new day starts fresh from number 1.

## Components

Think of the system as four simple screens that work together in the office.

### 1. Home

A starting page with the city and office logos. Staff pick the screen they need: a window, the supervisor page, or the public display.

### 2. Counter (window / teller)

Used at each window. Counters 1 and 2 serve clients; Counter 3 is the Releasing Window.

- Shows the number currently being served, and whether it is regular or priority.
- Counters 1 and 2: buttons to call the next regular client, call the next priority client, and then either finish the client or send them to the Releasing Window.
- Releasing Window: a list of clients waiting for their documents. Staff tap a number to call that client, can call again or put them back on the list if they do not come, and mark the document as released.
- Keyboard shortcuts for faster work (Enter or Space to call next, P for priority, S to send to releasing, R to call again, D for done or released).
- Shows what the other windows are serving.

Staff wait here until a supervisor starts the queue for the day.

### 3. Admin (supervisor)

Used by the person in charge of the day’s operations.

- Start the queue so windows can begin calling.
- Reset the queue (with a confirmation) to clear everything and start again from 1.
- Rename windows (for example, “Window A” or “Civil Registry”).
- Turn a window on or off when it is not in use.
- Watch live counts and a full activity log.

### 4. Display (waiting-area screen)

Meant for a large screen facing the public.

- City and office logos.
- Live clock.
- A clear card for each window: name, number now serving, and whether it is a priority call.
- A "For Release" list of numbers waiting for their documents.
- Voice call-out when a new number is assigned or a document is ready.
- Totals for people served and windows currently serving.

## Benefits

**For the public**

- Clear, visible order — no crowding the windows to ask “who is next?”
- Priority clients have their own line and are easy to recognize on the screen (P numbers).
- Voice announcements help people who are seated, elderly, or not looking at the screen at that moment.
- Less confusion and fewer disputes in the waiting area.

**For front-line staff**

- Simple, large buttons and shortcuts — call, serve, done.
- No need to keep a paper list or shout numbers across the room.
- Each window always knows what the others are doing.
- One client at a time keeps the work focused.

**For supervisors and the office**

- One place to open or close the day’s queue and to pause unused windows.
- A record of who was called, at which window, and when.
- Daily counts of regular vs. priority clients served — useful for reporting and planning.
- PIN protection so only authorized staff can change queue settings.
- Designed for a local office setup: it runs on the office network and does not depend on the public internet.

**For the city office’s image**

- A professional, branded waiting experience with Maasin City and Civil Registrar logos.
- A calmer, more organized hall that matches the standards of public service.
